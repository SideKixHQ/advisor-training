Module 1 · Brand Training

The SideKix Mission & Advisor Role

Why we exist, and what an Advisor is actually here to do.

Learning objective
By the end of this training, you'll be able to explain the purpose of SideKix, understand your role within the community, and demonstrate how to create a member experience built around empowerment, practical guidance, and action.
Waypoint 01

Why SideKix exists

Millions of people have ideas for businesses, but most never take the first step. The problem isn't a lack of ambition — it's access.

People struggle to move forward because they lack:

  • A clear path forward
  • Trusted guidance
  • Practical resources
  • Confidence to take action
  • A community that supports their journey

SideKix exists to close that gap — connecting potential entrepreneurs and business owners with experienced Advisors who help turn uncertainty into progress.

Waypoint 02

The Advisor's role

Advisors are the heartbeat of SideKix. Your role is not to build the business for the member — it's to help members become capable of building it themselves.

The SideKix Advisor North Star
"Advisors are experienced guides who transfer clarity, confidence, and capability to the builders of tomorrow."
Clarity

Help members understand

  • What they are building
  • Who they are serving
  • What decisions come next
  • What steps come next
Confidence

Help members believe

  • Their idea is worth exploring
  • They can learn from challenges
  • They can move forward despite uncertainty
Capability

Help members grow

  • Gain relevant skills
  • Access the right resources
  • Make better decisions independently
Waypoint 03

The SideKix member experience

Every interaction should leave a member feeling three things.

Supported
"I have someone who understands my journey."
Empowered
"I believe I can take the next step."
Equipped
"I know what I need to do next."

SideKix isn't about academic theory, status, or ego. Members don't need someone to tell them how impressive they are — they need someone who can share real-world experience and help them execute.

Waypoint 04

The Advisor mindset

We do not rescue. We do not control the outcome. We empower members to take ownership of their journey.

We do
  • Ask questions before giving answers
  • Share practical experience
  • Encourage progress
  • Provide honest feedback
  • Help members create action plans
We do not
  • Judge ideas
  • Take control of the member's journey
  • Guarantee success
  • Create dependency
  • Make the experience about ourselves

Module 2 · Mindset, Boundaries & Session Anatomy

The Advisor Framework

From having the answer, to running a session that gets someone to their own.

Learning objective
By the end of this training, you'll understand the shift from giving answers to guiding, know the boundaries of the Advisor role, and be able to run the five step SideKix session from preparation through a clear action plan.
Waypoint 01

The Advisor shift: Expert → Guide

The goal is not to create dependency. The goal is to empower members to make better decisions — including the ones you won't be in the room for.

Traditional Consultant
"Here is what you should do."
Solves the problem in front of them. The member leaves with an answer, but not necessarily the ability to get the next one themselves.
SideKix Advisor
"Let's understand your goal, explore your options, and identify the next best step."
Solves the problem with them. The member leaves with a decision they own — and a little more confidence to face the next one.
Waypoint 02

What Advisors can do

A great Advisor experience doesn't happen by accident. To keep it consistent, trusted, and premium for every member, Advisors follow a shared operating framework — starting with what's in bounds.

Lead structured sessions
  • Create focused conversations
  • Help members identify challenges and opportunities
  • Guide discussions toward action
Share practical wisdom
  • Share real-world experience
  • Explain lessons learned from successes and failures
  • Provide insight based on personal expertise
Empower members
  • Ask thoughtful questions
  • Help members think through decisions
  • Provide resources and recommendations
  • Encourage ownership of their journey
Build trust
  • Listen actively
  • Create a safe environment
  • Support members without judgment
Waypoint 03

What Advisors cannot do

The goal is guidance, not control — and SideKix exists to serve the member first.

Take members off platform
  • Exchange personal payment arrangements
  • Move conversations outside SideKix to avoid platform processes
  • Create private business relationships outside the Advisor role
Impose decisions
  • Tell members exactly what they must do
  • Make business decisions on the member's behalf
  • Replace the member's ownership of their journey
Use SideKix as lead generation
  • Pitch external consulting services
  • Sell unrelated products or programs
  • Use member relationships for personal business gain
Waypoint 04

Anatomy of a great session

Every SideKix Advisor session follows the same five steps.

Step 1 · Prepare

Before the session

Great sessions begin before the meeting starts.

  • Review the member profile
  • Review previous notes
  • Understand their goals and challenges
  • Prepare questions that will create value
Goal: enter the conversation informed and ready.
Step 2 · Build trust

First 3–5 minutes

The first few minutes set the tone.

  • Create a welcoming environment
  • Build rapport
  • Understand what matters most to the member
"What would make this session a win for you?"
Goal: make the member feel heard and supported.
Step 3 · Discover

High listening

Understand before recommending. Great Advisors listen more than they talk.

"What have you tried so far?" · "What's the biggest obstacle right now?" · "What does success look like six months from now?"
Goal: diagnose the actual need, not just the surface problem.
Step 4 · Guide

Practical direction

Once you understand the situation, provide guidance that:

  • Simplifies complex problems
  • Shares relevant experience
  • Provides realistic options
  • Helps the member make informed decisions
The Advisor provides the map. The member chooses the path. Goal: create clarity without taking control.
Step 5 · Action plan & close

High alignment

Before closing:

  • Identify 1–3 specific next steps
  • Confirm the member understands the plan
  • Make sure actions are realistic
  • Encourage continued progress

After the session: log notes, document progress, capture context for future sessions.

Goal: the member leaves knowing exactly what to do next.
Waypoint 05

The Insight-Action Framework

When a member brings a complex challenge, use this framework to simplify the problem and provide direction.

Step 1 · Insight

What do you see?

Start by naming the key observation: what pattern you notice, what may be holding the member back, what looks like the biggest opportunity.

Member says"I have a great product, but nobody is buying."
"It sounds like the biggest challenge may not be the product itself, but making sure the right customers understand the value of what you offer."
Step 2 · Explanation

Why does it matter?

Help the member understand why the issue exists, how it impacts their progress, and what principle or lesson applies.

"Many businesses struggle because they focus on what they sell instead of clearly communicating who they help and what problem they solve."
Step 3 · Action

What should they do next?

Turn understanding into execution: a specific next step, a realistic action item, a way to measure progress.

"Your next step is to identify your ideal customer and write a one-sentence statement explaining the problem you solve for them."
Insight → Explanation → Action

Great Advisors don't just provide answers. They help members understand what is happening, why it matters, and what to do next.

Waypoint 06

Avoid the information dump

A common mistake: providing too much information. More information doesn't always create more value.

Overwhelming
20 ideas. 10 resources. 5 strategies.
A member who receives all of this may leave more overwhelmed than when they started.
Focused
One important insight. One clear explanation. One to three actionable steps.
Value over volume — this is what actually creates momentum.

Module 3 · Advisor Ethics, Integrity & Member Trust

Protecting the Member Relationship

"Your influence is a privilege. Use it to empower, not persuade."

Learning objective
By the end of this training, you'll understand your responsibility as a trusted guide and demonstrate the ability to provide ethical, unbiased support that prioritizes the member's best interest while maintaining the trust, safety, and integrity of the SideKix community.
Waypoint 01

The responsibility of being an Advisor

Becoming a SideKix Advisor means members are trusting you with something deeply personal: their goals, ideas, challenges, and decisions. For many members, starting a business may represent:

  • A dream they have carried for years
  • A chance to create independence
  • A way to support their family
  • A personal mission or purpose

That trust is a privilege. With influence comes responsibility. The role of an Advisor is not to convince members to follow your path — it's to help members think clearly, make informed decisions, and take ownership of their journey.

Influence
"Here is what I am seeing. What do you make of it?"
Influence helps someone think clearly and reach their own decision. The member ends up somewhere they chose.
Persuasion
"Here is what you should do, and here is why you are wrong."
Persuasion pushes toward one outcome the Advisor already picked. The member ends up somewhere you chose.

Both use the same trust. Only one leaves the decision with the member.

Waypoint 02

The SideKix Advisor Integrity Standard

Every Advisor interaction should be guided by three questions.

Question 1

Is this in the member's best interest?

Before recommending an action, resource, or strategy, ask: does this genuinely help the member? Am I recommending this because it fits their situation — or because it benefits me?

Question 2

Am I helping the member think, or telling them what to think?

Advisors provide: perspective, experience, options, guidance.

Advisors do not: pressure, manipulate, push personal beliefs, or make decisions for members.

Question 3 · The integrity test

Would I give this same advice if I had nothing to gain?

If the answer is no, reconsider the recommendation.

Waypoint 03

Managing conflicts of interest

A conflict of interest occurs when personal interests could influence professional guidance.

SituationAn Advisor owns a marketing agency. A member needs marketing help.
Not appropriate
"You should hire my agency. I can get you started today."
Appropriate
"Marketing may be an area where you need support. I can share some general guidance, and SideKix has resources that may help you explore your options."

SideKix standard: Advisors may share expertise. Advisors may not use member relationships to create personal business opportunities. This protects members, Advisors, and the SideKix community.

Waypoint 04

Providing guidance without pushing personal opinions

Your experience is a perspective, not a universal answer. A strategy that worked for you may not work for every member — different industries, resources, goals, risk levels, and life circumstances all matter.

✗ "You need to quit your job and go all in."

✓ "One option some entrepreneurs consider is transitioning full-time. Let's explore what approach makes sense based on your goals, resources, and situation."

Use language that creates perspective instead of directives:

  • "One approach you could consider is..."
  • "I have seen this work when..."
  • "One risk to think about is..."
  • "Let's explore whether this fits your situation."
Waypoint 05

Protecting member information

Members trust Advisors with personal information — business ideas, challenges, financial concerns, personal goals, strategic decisions.

  • Keep member information confidential
  • Only discuss member information when appropriate
  • Avoid sharing stories that identify members
  • Respect privacy inside and outside the platform

A member's story is not yours to tell. Even positive stories require permission before sharing.

Waypoint 06

Respecting different backgrounds & experiences

SideKix exists to support entrepreneurs from all backgrounds and stages — different financial situations, education levels, career experiences, personal responsibilities, cultural perspectives, and definitions of success.

An Advisor's role is not to judge the path. An Advisor's role is to help the member build the path that fits them.

Not this
"Here is what success should look like."
This
"What does success look like for you?"
Waypoint 07

Knowing when to refer to a specialist

Knowing when to refer someone is a sign of professionalism, not weakness. Recommend specialized support when a situation involves:

  • Legal advice
  • Tax decisions
  • Accounting matters
  • Mental health concerns
  • Complex financial decisions
  • Industry-specific compliance
Don't say
"I can't help you."
Say
"This is an area where getting specialized guidance would be valuable. I can help you identify what questions to ask and what next steps to consider."

Module 4 · Scenario-Based Training

Navigating Real-World Conversations

A great Advisor doesn't need every conversation to be perfect. A great Advisor knows how to create clarity from uncertainty.

Learning objective
By the end of this training, you'll be able to manage challenging member interactions by applying empathy, active listening, structured questioning, and healthy boundaries to keep sessions productive, supportive, and action-oriented.
Waypoint 01

The reality of Advisor sessions

Not every member arrives with a clear goal, organized thoughts, or confidence. Some are overwhelmed by too many ideas, unsure where to start, afraid of making the wrong decision, looking for someone to solve the problem for them, or unsure how to communicate what they need.

Covered in Module 1

The Advisor commitment not to rescue, not to control the outcome, and not to take ownership of the member's journey is set in Module 1. Everything below is how that holds up when a conversation gets difficult.

What that requires here
  • Create psychological safety
  • Understand the real challenge
  • Guide the conversation
  • Help the member take ownership of the next step
Waypoint 02

Five real-world scenarios

The same five situations come up again and again. Here's how to handle each.

Scenario 1 · The over-talker

Turning chaos into clarity

A member arrives overwhelmed and starts sharing every business idea they have, every problem they're facing, every concern about the future. The conversation loses focus.

Don't shut them down — they're often thinking out loud to organize their thoughts. Listen, acknowledge, identify patterns, then gently create structure.

"It sounds like there are several things you're trying to solve right now. Let's narrow this down. What's the biggest challenge preventing you from moving forward today?"
Goal: move from "everything feels important" to "this is the one thing we need to focus on first."
Scenario 2 · The silent member

Creating psychological safety

A member gives short answers — "I don't know," "Maybe," "I'm not sure." They may be nervous, unsure what to ask, or afraid their idea isn't good enough.

Don't fill the silence by talking more — silence gives members space to think. Use open-ended questions, and validate before advising.

"What made you interested in starting this?" · "Where do you feel the most stuck right now?" · "What would you like to accomplish by the end of this conversation?"
"A lot of entrepreneurs start exactly where you are. Let's figure out the next step together."
Goal: create an environment where the member feels safe enough to be honest.
Scenario 3 · The confused member

Simplifying complexity

Member says"I need help with everything. I don't know where to start."

They may be dealing with too many priorities, information overload, or fear of choosing the wrong path. Don't provide a massive checklist — complex problems require clarity before action.

"What's the biggest obstacle right now?" · "What have you already tried?" · "What would create the most progress in the next 30 days?"

Then apply the Insight-Action Framework: identify the biggest issue, explain why it matters, create 1–3 simple next steps. Close with alignment: "How does that approach feel to you?"

Goal: turn "I don't know where to start" into "I know exactly what my next step is."
Scenario 4 · Boundary violations

Protecting the community

A member asks: "Can I just pay you directly?" · "Can we continue this conversation outside the app?" · "Can I message you whenever I need advice?"

The policy itself is in Module 3. What this scenario is for is the wording, because holding the boundary without making the member feel rejected is the hard part.

"I appreciate you wanting to continue working together. To protect both Advisors and members, all sessions and payments need to stay within SideKix. The best next step would be scheduling another session through the platform."
Scenario 5 · The member who wants answers

Avoiding the rescue trap

Member says"Just tell me what I should do."

Avoid immediately giving the answer. Ask first, then guide: share experience, explain tradeoffs, offer recommendations, and let the member decide.

"Before I give my perspective, can I understand how you're thinking about the decision?"
Goal: the member leaves saying "I know how to think through this" — not "My Advisor told me what to do."

Module 5 · The Art of Asking

The Art of Asking

The difference isn't how much you know. It's how well you unlock the thinking of the person in front of you.

Learning objective
By the end of this training, you'll be able to use strategic questioning techniques to uncover the true challenges, motivations, and opportunities behind a member's situation, enabling more personalized guidance and better outcomes.
Waypoint 01

The Advisor's superpower

Members rarely come with the complete problem. They come with symptoms.

A symptom is what the problem looks like from the outside. A root cause is the actual reason it is happening. Solving a symptom feels productive and changes very little, because the reason it appeared is still there.

Member says"I need help with marketing."

The real issue may be:

  • They don't understand their customer
  • They're afraid of selling
  • Their offer is unclear
  • They have no process
  • They lack confidence

The Advisor's job is not to immediately solve the stated problem. The Advisor's job is to discover the real problem.

Waypoint 02

The Discovery Principle

Before advising: seek to understand before seeking to solve.

Situation → Challenge → Root Cause → Opportunity → Action
Waypoint 03

The five levels of Advisor questions

Each level takes the conversation one layer deeper.

Level 1 · Understanding

Understand the current situation

"Tell me about where you are today." · "What have you already tried?" · "What does your business currently look like?"
Level 2 · Challenge

Identify obstacles

"What's the biggest thing preventing progress right now?" · "Where are you feeling stuck?" · "What feels hardest about this?"
Level 3 · Root cause

Move beyond symptoms

Member says"I can't get customers."

✗ Weak response: "You need more marketing."

Better: "Who is your ideal customer?" · "How are customers currently finding you?" · "What happens when someone learns about your business?"
Level 4 · Vision

Understand motivation

"Why does this business matter to you?" · "What are you hoping this creates for your life?" · "What does success look like?"
Understanding the "why" helps Advisors provide better guidance.
Level 5 · Commitment

Create ownership

"What feels like the right next step?" · "What action are you willing to commit to?" · "What support do you need to make that happen?"
Waypoint 04

The power of the pause

Great Advisors are comfortable with silence. A common mistake is answering silence with more talking.

Silence is valuable because it gives members the space to reflect and develop their own insight. Filling it takes that space back. Given a few seconds, members think deeper, answer more honestly, and often arrive at something they did not know they believed.

A powerful Advisor question followed by silence can create more progress than a 10-minute explanation.

Waypoint 05

Avoid leading questions

Avoid questions that already contain the answer. The goal is discovery, not confirmation.

Poor
"Don't you think you should focus on social media?"
Better
"What channels have you explored for reaching customers?"

Module 6 · Member Journey & Personalization

Meeting Members Where They Are

Two questions, not two models: where is this member, and who is this member?

Learning objective
By the end of this training, you'll be able to place a member on the member journey, understand the six dimensions that make their situation their own, and adapt your guidance to both.
Waypoint 01

The myth of the universal path

One of the biggest mistakes Advisors can make is assuming their personal experience represents the "right" path for everyone. A strategy that worked for one member may fail for another because every member has different:

  • Goals
  • Motivations
  • Resources
  • Responsibilities
  • Experience levels
  • Risk tolerance
  • Personal definitions of success

The Advisor's job is not to create more people like themselves. The Advisor's job is to help each member build the path that makes sense for them.

Waypoint 02

The Four Stages of the Member Journey

Four stages, each with its own needs, challenges, and focus.

Stage 1 · The idea stage

Has an idea, may lack confidence

The member is exploring possibilities, may lack confidence, and may not know where to begin.

"Is my idea good enough?" · "Who would buy this?" · "Where do I start?"

Advisor focus: create clarity. Help them define the problem they're solving, identify who they serve, test assumptions, and take the first small action.

Avoid jumping into advanced topics like scaling, hiring, or complex financial models.
Stage 2 · The validation stage

Testing the idea against reality

The member has an idea they want to test, is researching customers, and is trying to understand demand.

Common challenges: fear of rejection · uncertainty about customers · building something nobody wants

Advisor focus: help them learn from the market. Guide them to talk with customers, gather feedback, test assumptions, and improve their offer.

Key question: "What evidence do we have that this solves a real problem?"
Stage 3 · The building stage

Taking action, creating systems

The member has started taking action, may have early customers, and is creating systems.

Common challenges: lack of focus · too many priorities · limited resources

Advisor focus: help them prioritize. Guide them toward consistent execution, customer acquisition, and improving operations.

Key question: "What is the one thing that would create the biggest impact right now?"
Stage 4 · The growth stage

Operating, scaling, optimizing

The member has an operating business and wants to increase revenue and improve efficiency.

Common challenges: scaling challenges · hiring · processes · financial decisions

Advisor focus: help them identify bottlenecks. Guide them toward better systems, strategic decisions, and sustainable growth.

Waypoint 03

Understanding the person behind the business

1 · Goals

What are they trying to achieve?

Not every member wants the same outcome — a side income, a lifestyle business, a full-time career transition, community impact, financial independence, a scalable company, or creative freedom.

"What are you hoping this business creates for you?"
2 · Motivation

Why does this matter?

A member motivated by freedom may need different guidance than someone motivated by rapid growth.

"Why is this important to you?" · "Why now?" · "What would achieving this goal change for you?"
3 · Experience level

Where are they starting?

A first-time member and a member who has already run a business should not receive the same guidance.

A first-time member may need: confidence, fundamentals, validation, clear first steps.

An experienced member may need: strategic feedback, optimization, growth support, new perspectives.

"Have you built or operated a business before?"
4 · Available time

What capacity do they have?

Advice must fit reality. A member working full-time with family responsibilities may have 5 hours a week; another may have 40. A recommendation that ignores capacity creates frustration.

"How much time can you realistically dedicate each week?" · "What responsibilities do you need to balance?"
5 · Financial constraints

What resources are available?

Never assume they can hire professionals, purchase software, invest thousands of dollars, or have savings available.

"What resources do you currently have available to support this goal?"
6 · Risk tolerance

What level of uncertainty fits them?

Some members are comfortable taking significant risks. Others need a more gradual approach. Neither is wrong.

"How comfortable are you with making this transition?" · "What would feel like a responsible next step?"
Waypoint 04

Avoiding Advisor assumptions

Great Advisors challenge their own assumptions.

"Everyone wants to scale."

Reality

Some members want more freedom, more flexibility, a sustainable business, or additional income. Scaling is an option, not the definition of success.

"Everyone wants funding."

Reality

Many businesses grow through revenue, customers, partnerships, or bootstrapping. Funding is a tool, not a requirement.

"Everyone wants to quit their job."

Reality

Many successful members start while maintaining employment. A side business may be the right strategy.

"Everyone wants a traditional business."

Reality

Members may pursue freelancing, consulting, digital products, local businesses, creative ventures, community-based businesses, or technology companies. The model should match the member's goals.

Waypoint 05

The Advisor Adaptation Model

Before giving advice, ask yourself these three questions.

01

Where are they?

What stage are they currently in?

02

What is blocking them?

What challenge is preventing progress?

03

What is the next best step?

What action creates momentum?

Waypoint 06

The most important SideKix question

"What does success look like for YOU?"

This question prevents Advisors from projecting their own goals onto members. A member's definition of success may be:

  • Replacing their income
  • Building something meaningful
  • Creating flexibility
  • Supporting their family
  • Proving an idea works
  • Building a company

The Advisor's job is to help them achieve their version of success.

Practice

Module 7 · Trust, Guidance & Relationship

Trust, Guidance & the Member Relationship

People are more open to guidance when they feel heard first, and they stay for the relationship, not the session.

Learning objective
By the end of this training, you'll be able to offer guidance without taking ownership away from a member, build trust through consistency and genuine care, and hold that trust through uncertainty, disagreement, and difficult moments.
Waypoint 01

Understand before you influence

Every member enters a conversation with their own experiences, beliefs, goals, fears, assumptions, and prior decisions. Your job isn't to immediately correct their thinking — it's to understand the thinking behind it.

Member says"I think I need to spend $10,000 on advertising."
An inexperienced Advisor
"No, that's a bad idea. You should not do that."
A SideKix Advisor
"Help me understand what led you to that decision. What are you hoping that investment will accomplish?"

The second approach creates understanding before guidance.

Waypoint 02

Create alignment before recommendations

Before offering a recommendation, make sure you understand what the member is trying to accomplish, why it matters to them, what they've already considered, and what constraints they're facing.

"Would it be helpful if I shared a perspective based on my experience?" · "Can I share what I'm noticing?" · "Would you like my thoughts on a possible next step?"
Advice without alignment feels like
  • Judgment
  • Criticism
  • Someone taking control
Advice with alignment feels like
  • Partnership
  • Support
  • Guidance

The goal is not to win the conversation. The goal is to help the member move forward.

Waypoint 03

The SideKix Guidance Formula

1 · Understand
"Help me understand how you are thinking about this."
2 · Connect
"Based on what you shared, here is what I am noticing."
3 · Offer perspective
"One thing I have seen work in similar situations is..."
4 · Empower
"What feels like the best next step for you?"

Three ways this goes wrong

Solving too quickly

The Advisor hears a problem and jumps to the solution. The real problem may be different, the member may not feel understood, and the recommendation may not fit their situation.

Making it about your experience

"I built a company, and this is what I did." Every member has different resources, customers, timing, and goals. Experience should inform guidance, not replace understanding.

Correcting instead of coaching

✗ "That won't work."

✓ "Help me understand how you arrived at that approach. Can we explore another perspective together?"

Waypoint 04

The Trust Equation

Trust is built through four behaviors.

1 · Consistency

Members should know what to expect

  • Showing up prepared
  • Following through
  • Remembering previous conversations
  • Maintaining professionalism
A member should feel: "My Advisor understands my journey."
2 · Genuine Care

Investing versus just completing a session

  • Remembering their goals
  • Asking about previous action steps
  • Celebrating progress
  • Recognizing challenges

✗ "Did you complete the task?"

✓ "How did the customer conversations go? What did you learn?"

3 · Creating Wins

Not every win looks like revenue

Wins can be:

  • Taking the first step
  • Validating an idea
  • Having a difficult conversation
  • Learning from failure
  • Completing a goal
Help members recognize their progress.
4 · Belonging

SideKix is a community, not just a tool

  • Encouraging participation
  • Connecting members to resources
  • Recognizing their journey
  • Reinforcing that challenges are normal
A member should feel: "I am part of something bigger than myself."

What builds loyalty, and what breaks it

Builds loyalty
  • Remembering details from previous conversations
  • Following up on commitments
  • Celebrating small wins
  • Being honest and transparent
  • Providing value beyond expectations
  • Making members feel seen
Breaks loyalty
  • Making sessions feel transactional
  • Only focusing on problems
  • Forgetting previous conversations
  • Talking more than listening
  • Making members feel judged
  • Treating every member the same
Waypoint 05

Three moments in the relationship

Every Advisor should understand three moments.

Moment 1 · First interaction

"Do I trust you?"

The member is asking: "Does this person understand me?" · "Are they here to help me?" · "Is this a safe environment?"
Advisor focus: build rapport, listen, understand their story.
Moment 2 · Middle of the journey

"Am I making progress?"

The member is asking: "Am I moving forward?" · "Am I doing the right things?" · "Is this worth continuing?"
Advisor focus: celebrate progress, adjust goals, maintain momentum.
Moment 3 · Long-term relationship

"Do I belong here?"

The member is asking: "Is SideKix part of my journey?" · "Do I have people supporting me?" · "Do I want to continue growing here?"
Advisor focus: strengthen connection, encourage community participation, continue adding value.
Waypoint 06

When you don't know the answer

The wrong approach — "I don't know" — creates uncertainty without support. The SideKix approach moves through three steps.

1 · Acknowledge

Be transparent

"That's a great question. I want to be thoughtful with my answer because I don't want to give you the wrong direction."
2 · Commit

Reassure the member you will help

"This is something I want to look into further so we can make sure you have the right information."
3 · Collaborate

Move toward a solution

"Let's talk through what we know today, what information we need, and what the next best step is."
Waypoint 07

Managing disagreement with members

Sometimes members will disagree with your recommendation, defend an idea you question, reject your feedback, or become frustrated. The Advisor's goal is not to convince — it's to understand.

Step 1 · Recognize their perspective

Don't immediately challenge.

"I understand why that approach feels appealing."
This doesn't mean you agree. It means you're showing you understand.
Step 2 · Explore the reason behind it
"What makes this approach feel like the right choice?" · "What outcome are you hoping this creates?" · "What concerns do you have about another approach?"
Step 3 · Share your perspective

Use experience, not authority.

✗ "That won't work."

✓ "One thing I would consider is..." or "A risk I have seen with that approach is..."

Step 4 · Align on the next step
"Given everything we discussed, what feels like the best path forward?"
Waypoint 08

Handling emotional conversations

Entrepreneurship is personal. Members may feel fear, frustration, embarrassment, doubt, or disappointment. Recognize the emotion before solving the problem.

Member says"I feel like I am failing."
The wrong approach
"You just need a better strategy."
This skips the human moment entirely.
The SideKix approach
"It sounds like this has been frustrating and exhausting."
Acknowledge, then normalize ("Many entrepreneurs experience moments like this"), then guide ("Let's look at what's happening and identify one thing we can improve").

Module 8 · Accountability, Momentum & Breakthroughs

Momentum & Breakthroughs

A session isn't measured by how much was shared. It's measured by what happens after.

Learning objective
By the end of this training, you'll be able to turn insight into commitment, handle a missed action item with curiosity rather than judgment, and create the conditions for a member to reach their own breakthrough.
Waypoint 01

The Momentum Framework

Every Advisor interaction should move through three stages.

Stage 1 · Clarify the goal

Make sure the member knows what they're trying to accomplish

"What outcome are you trying to achieve?" · "Why is this important right now?" · "How will you know you made progress?"

✗ Vague: "I want to grow my business."

✓ Clear: "I want to identify my first 10 potential customers and schedule 5 conversations this month."

Stage 2 · Create the commitment

Turn ideas into commitments

A good action step is specific (what exactly will they do?), realistic (can they complete it given their situation?), and time-based (when will it happen?).

✗ Weak: "Work on marketing."

✓ Strong: "By Friday, create a list of 20 potential customers and reach out to five of them for feedback."

Stage 3 · Build accountability

Accountability is support, not pressure

"What are you committing to?" · "What could prevent you from completing it?" · "What support do you need?"
Asking "What could get in the way of completing this?" lets the Advisor help remove obstacles before they happen.
Waypoint 02

The SideKix Progress Loop

Every Advisor session should create this cycle.

Understand
Where is the member today?
→
Prioritize
What matters most right now?
→
Act
What step will they take?
→
Reflect
What did they learn?
→
Improve
What's the next step?
...and back to Understand for the next session.
Waypoint 03

Handling missed commitments

Members will sometimes not complete their action items. The Advisor should not create shame.

Avoid
"Why didn't you do it?"
Use curiosity
"What got in the way?"

Then explore:

  • Was the goal too large?
  • Was there a missing resource?
  • Did priorities change?
  • Did they lose confidence?

The goal is learning, not punishment.

Motivation alone
"You can do anything!"
Motivation fades. It feels good in the session and changes nothing by Wednesday.
Progress instead
"Let's identify the smallest action you can take this week that moves you closer to your goal."
Progress creates confidence. Small wins build belief in a way encouragement cannot.
Waypoint 04

The SideKix Breakthrough Model

A breakthrough is not a great piece of advice, a motivational speech, or an Advisor proving their expertise. It is a meaningful shift in how a member understands their situation, their possibilities, or their next actions. The member moves from "I am stuck" to "I understand what is happening and what I can do next."

People usually struggle not because they lack information, but because they are working from incomplete information, limiting assumptions, fear, or overwhelming complexity. The Advisor does not insert the insight. The Advisor creates the conditions where the member can find it.

That shift happens in three ways.

Stage 1 · Awareness breakthrough

"I understand my problem differently."

Many members initially identify the wrong problem — they focus on symptoms instead of root causes.

Member says"I need more customers."

Initial assumption: "I have a marketing problem."

Through discovery: "You may not have a marketing problem yet. You may have a customer clarity problem. Before spending money to reach more people, let's understand exactly who you are trying to reach and why they would choose you."
What changed: the member moved from "I need more attention" to "I need a clearer understanding of my customer."

How Advisors create awareness:

"What makes you believe that is the biggest challenge?" · "How do you know this is the problem?" · "What evidence do you have?" · "What else could be contributing to this?"
Stage 2 · Possibility breakthrough

"I can actually see a path forward."

Many members are limited by perceived barriers: "I need more money first." "I need everything figured out." "I need to be an expert." "I need the perfect plan."

Member says"I cannot start until I have a complete business plan."
"A business plan can be valuable, but at this stage your biggest opportunity may be learning whether customers actually want this solution. What if the first step is not building the entire business, but having five conversations with potential customers?"
What changed: the member moved from "I need everything figured out before I start" to "I can take a small step and learn."

How Advisors create possibility — help members separate reality from assumptions:

"Is that a fact, or is that a belief?" · "What would you do if that obstacle was smaller than you thought?" · "What is one possible way forward?"
Stage 3 · Action breakthrough

"I know exactly what to do next."

Many members are overwhelmed because they see the entire journey instead of the next step. The Advisor's job is to reduce complexity.

Member says"I need to figure out my entire business."
"Your next step is not figuring everything out. Your next step is identifying your first customer group and having five conversations to learn what they need."
What changed: the member moved from "I have too much to do" to "I know my next move."
Waypoint 05

Create conditions, not conclusions

A common mistake is trying to force breakthroughs. These approaches create resistance.

Avoid
  • "You just need more confidence."
  • "You are thinking about this wrong."
  • "Here is the answer."
Instead
  • Ask
  • Listen
  • Reflect
  • Explore
  • Guide

The member should feel "I discovered something important" — not "My Advisor told me something."

Waypoint 06

The Breakthrough Conversation Framework

Step 1 · Listen for patterns

Pay attention to repeated concerns, contradictions, assumptions, emotional signals, and areas of uncertainty.

Step 2 · Reflect back

Help the member hear their own thinking.

"I am noticing that you have spent a lot of time improving the product, but we have not yet explored who the ideal customer is. Does that feel accurate?"
Step 3 · Explore new perspectives
"What if the challenge is not what you initially thought?" · "What else could be true?" · "What would you do differently if you had more clarity?"
Step 4 · Convert insight into action

A breakthrough without action fades. Always close with:

"What did you learn?" · "What changed in your thinking?" · "What is your next step?"

Module 9 · Notes, Community & Advisor Growth

Continuity, Community & Growth

Great Advisors create great conversations. Elite Advisors create continuity.

Learning objective
By the end of this training, you'll be able to document a session so the next person can pick it up cleanly, contribute to the community in a way that builds credibility, and use feedback as information rather than judgment.
Waypoint 01

The SideKix Session Note Framework

Every session note should include five key areas.

1 · Context

Understanding the member's goal

"What is the member working toward?"

Capture their current objective, business stage, motivation, and desired outcome.

✗ "Talked about business idea."

✓ "Member is exploring launching a local meal-prep business and wants to validate customer demand before investing in equipment."

2 · Challenge

Identifying the real obstacle

"What is preventing progress right now?"

Capture current barriers, concerns, and areas where they need support — unclear target customer, lack of confidence, limited time, difficulty prioritizing, need for specific expertise.

Avoid documenting assumptions. Write what was observed or shared.
3 · Guidance

Capturing what was discussed

"What insights or direction were provided?"

✗ "I told them they need better marketing."

✓ "Discussed the importance of defining the target customer before selecting marketing channels. Reviewed customer discovery as the next step."

4 · Commitment

Documenting the action plan

"What action will the member take next?"

Good commitments are clear, realistic, and time-based.

✗ "Work on marketing."

✓ "Member will identify 10 potential customers and conduct five customer interviews before the next session."

5 · Follow-Up

Creating continuity

"What should happen next?"

Document recommended next steps, follow-up topics, resources needed, and potential future support.

"Next session should review customer feedback and determine whether the current offer needs adjustment."
Waypoint 02

Writing professional session notes

Session notes should be objective, respectful, and action-oriented.

Objective

Document facts, not personal opinions

✗ "Member has unrealistic expectations."

✓ "Member is currently targeting a large market opportunity but has not yet completed customer validation."

Respectful

Remember: members may see this

The member may eventually see their information. Write with professionalism and respect.

Action-oriented

Focus on progress

The purpose of notes is not to record what happened. The purpose is to improve what happens next.

Waypoint 03

The continuity test

Before submitting notes, ask yourself these three questions.

  • Could another Advisor understand this member's journey?
  • Could the AI Journey Map use this information to provide better guidance?
  • Would the member feel respected reading this?

If the answer is yes to all three, the notes are serving their purpose.

Waypoint 05

Contribute value

The community is where Advisors demonstrate their expertise beyond scheduled sessions.

  • Answering member questions
  • Sharing practical lessons learned
  • Participating in discussions
  • Supporting other members
  • Celebrating member wins
  • Sharing helpful resources

The goal is not self-promotion. The goal is contribution. Self-promotion asks people to notice you. Brand building earns attention through consistent value, which is why the Advisors members seek out are rarely the loudest ones.

Visibility creates awareness.
Value creates trust.
Consistency creates reputation.

"This person understands entrepreneurship and genuinely wants to help."
Waypoint 06

Feedback is a growth tool, not a judgment

The goal is not to receive perfect ratings. The goal is continuous improvement. Every piece of feedback provides information.

1 · What is the member telling me?

Listen beyond the rating

Feedback"The session felt rushed."

Possible meaning: too much information was provided, not enough discovery happened, the action plan was unclear, or the member needed more space to talk.

2 · Why might they feel this way?

Avoid becoming defensive

"Did I listen enough?" · "Did I understand their goal?" · "Did I provide too much information?" · "Did I create clear next steps?"
3 · What can I adjust?

Feedback should create action

If members say "I felt overwhelmed" — simplify recommendations, focus on fewer actions, slow down the conversation.

If members say "I wanted more direction" — provide clearer next steps, confirm understanding, create stronger action plans.

A weak Advisor believes
"My approach is already proven."
A strong Advisor believes
"I can always improve."

Negative feedback does not mean failure. It is information.

The wrong response
"The member just didn't understand."
The SideKix response
"What can I learn from this experience?"
